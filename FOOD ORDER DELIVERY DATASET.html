Conclusion¶

1. Cancellations are the biggest loss¶

85.2% of orders were delivered (22.92M), 9.8% were cancelled (2.62M) and 5.0% were still in transit (1.36M). About 1 in 10 orders is cancelled.

2. Traffic and driver availability do not explain the cancellations¶

  • Cancellation is 9.6-10.0% across low, medium and high traffic.
  • It is 9.8% with online drivers and 10.0% with offline drivers.
  • Average delivery time is about 37.5 minutes in every traffic level and for both driver states.

Neither factor drives cancellations or delivery time in this data, so the cause must be somewhere else.

3. Cities, items and payment methods are almost evenly split¶

  • Zagazig leads on revenue (3.91M) and Mansoura is last (3.76M), a gap of about 4%.
  • Shawarma is the top item (3.06M, about 11% of revenue) and Sushi is last (2.93M), also about a 4% gap.
  • Cash, wallet and credit card each carry about one third of revenue (spread under 1%).
  • Cancellation by city ranges from 9.3% (Zagazig) to 10.3% (Giza). Giza, Tanta and Mansoura are slightly above 10%.

These gaps are small and were not statistically tested, so no city, item or payment method clearly outperforms the others.

4. Demand is flat over time¶

  • Delivered orders are about 3,550 per hour (range 3,420-3,635), with no lunch or dinner peak, and about 5,700 per full day.
  • The Sunday/Monday are highest "spikes".

5. Offline-driver orders need an explanation¶

About 9.9% of orders (9,889) are linked to drivers marked Offline, and they still have normal delivery times. This probably means availability was recorded after the order, not at order time.

Recommendations¶

  1. Find the real cause of cancellations. Traffic and driver availability are ruled out. Test vehicle type, delivery distance, restaurant and item next. Cutting cancellations by 2 percentage points would recover about 0.5M.
  2. Clarify what Driver_Availability means. Check when it is recorded before using it for driver planning, since offline drivers are completing orders normally.
  3. Don't act on 4% gaps. City, item and payment differences are small. Run a significance test (for example chi-square on cancellation rate by city) or collect more data before reallocating inventory, drivers or marketing.
  4. Review Giza, Tanta and Mansoura. They have the highest cancellation rates (above 10%), but the differences are small, so confirm with a test first.
  5. Collect a longer period. Two weeks can't show weekly, monthly or seasonal patterns. Use at least three months.
  6. Report revenue on delivered orders only. Show cancelled and in-transit value as separate lines so headline revenue isn't overstated.

Limitations¶

The data covers only 1-16 June 2025, with partial first and last days, and looks synthetic. The analysis shows associations, not causes. Average delivery time includes all order statuses, so it should be recalculated for delivered orders only before it is used for decisions.
